# Analysis of Linked Health Data: Hospital Admissions & Mortality

## Overview

This project uses linked administrative hospital and mortality data to investigate hospital utilisation and time-to-event outcomes among adults aged 65 and older hospitalised for hip fracture between 2005 and 2014.

The project focuses on data linkage, data cleaning and validation, cohort construction, and analysis of healthcare utilisation outcomes.

## Objectives

* Construct a population-based cohort using linked hospital admissions and death registry data.
* Identify and resolve data quality issues, including duplicate records and inconsistent identifiers.
* Develop variables to distinguish individuals, admissions, transfers, and episodes of care.
* Analyse hospital utilisation outcomes, including transfers, length of stay, and mode of separation.
* Investigate time to readmission and death using survival analysis.
* Examine how accounting for hospital transfers can affect outcome estimates.

## Data Preparation and Methods

* **Data linkage:** Deterministic linkage of hospital admissions and mortality datasets.
* **Data cleaning:** Identification and resolution of duplicate records, inconsistent identifiers, and conflicting death dates using structured validation rules.
* **Variable creation:** Development of longitudinal sequencing variables to distinguish individuals, admissions, transfers, and episodes of care.
* **Outcome analysis:** Calculation of hospital utilisation measures and analysis of time to readmission and death.
* **Survival analysis:** Kaplan–Meier methods, including stratification by Charlson Comorbidity Index groups.

## Analytical Approach

1. Constructed the eligible study cohort from administrative health records.
2. Linked hospital admissions and mortality data.
3. Cleaned and validated records to improve data consistency.
4. Created variables to correctly sequence admissions, transfers, and episodes of care.
5. Analysed hospital utilisation outcomes.
6. Examined time-to-event outcomes, including readmission and death.
7. Investigated how the handling of hospital transfers could influence outcome estimates.

## Skills Demonstrated

Data linkage, data cleaning, data validation, cohort construction, feature and variable creation, exploratory data analysis, survival analysis, and analytical problem-solving.
